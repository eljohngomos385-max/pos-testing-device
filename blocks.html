Blocks · shared system · scratch file, not wired into the product

Blocks — one system, three blocks

Three blocks: the KPI strip, the breakdown list and the radial summary. In blocks-lab-picks.html each one wore its own reference's tokens — different greys, different radii, three different ideas about where colour goes. That is fine for judging a screenshot and useless for building a page.

So: the KPI card is the base. Its tone pair, its type scale and its radius are the system, unchanged. The other two were moved onto them. Everything is drawn with CSS and hand-written SVG — no library, no JS — so any of it can ship into the no-build-step app as it stands.

The page these land on is a three-column grid. A KPI is a half-height strip across the top; B and C are 1×1 and both stretch to one height, --b-h-block. Anything wider is a 1×2 or 1×3 of the same shell. That is the part worth getting right here — once a 1×1 has one height and one inside edge, building a page out of them is copy, paste, swap the rows.

The system

Six decisions. Every block below obeys all six; where one is broken it is broken on purpose and says so.

1 · Tone, and which way round

Page white, card #F9F9F9, gutter white. No border, no shadow. A tile on a card steps back to white — the same one-step move, flipped. It only works in a run: four grey cards read as a group, one alone reads as disabled.

27 13 12.5
2 · Three sizes, one step

Value 27/550/-.02em, label 13, meta 12.5. Label and meta are the same weight and nearly the same size — the value is the only thing that changes, and it changes a lot. Tight tracking on anything over 24px.

3 · Two colour jobs, never mixed

Status is green/red and colours one word inside an otherwise black sentence. Data is one hue in three depths, for shares of a total. A share is neither good nor bad, so it must not borrow the green.

4 · One number per card

A card lands on one figure. The KPI puts it at 27px under the label; the ring puts it in the hole; the breakdown puts it in the total row. Never twice on the same card.

5 · Name left, number right, change last

One .row rule serves the breakdown and the ring's legend: 36px, zebra banded, amounts tabular and right-aligned so the right edge is a straight column. Names truncate; rows never change height.

6 · The comparison is always spelled out

"in the last week", never a bare arrow with a tooltip. An em dash where there is nothing to compare against — not 0%, which claims a comparison that does not exist.

Kept from the reference

  • Grey cards, white page, white gutter, no border and no shadow. The opposite of the usual white-card-on-grey. Cheap, quiet, and it survives being printed.
  • The trend is a sentence and only one word is coloured. The rest is the same near-black as the label — not a muted tail, and not bold. Colour does all the emphasising on its own.
  • One size step, used twice. Label 13 → value 27 → meta 12.5. Tight tracking and a 550 weight on the number: at 27px a bold shouts, and default tracking looks like browser output.
  • Even 16px padding all round. The reference gave the card a 20px foot and 9/10 around the number; at this size that read as slack rather than generosity. Roughly 8px came off the card's height and nothing about it got tighter to read.

Changed on purpose

  • Green and red, not all-green. The reference never shows a down state, so it tells us nothing about a bad day. A cashier reading a red day needs the colour to do the work.
  • The ··· fades in on hover. Four always-on menu buttons across a row is furniture competing with the numbers.
  • The em dash state exists. The fourth card is the empty-day case: an em dash and a plain reason, no fake percentage.

What this one is

  • Three columns, nothing else. Name, amount, change. Every row is the same shape, so the eye runs straight down the right edge instead of parsing each line. That is the entire block.
  • The zebra band replaces the hairline. White on the grey card — the same one-step move as a card on the page, flipped, and already the system's raised tone. A band is easier to track across a wide row than a rule, and it costs no line weight. The stripes run 10px past the text on both sides so they read as bands, not highlights.
  • No icons. A circled glyph on every row is five identical marks saying nothing; the one that does mean something (a void) is then indistinguishable at a glance. The word "Voids" is the icon.
  • The total is the exception row: no band, a hairline above, semibold. It is the one figure the card exists to land on, which is also how it satisfies rule 4 without a 27px number — a breakdown that led with its total and then re-stated it at the bottom would print the same number twice.
  • Em dash where there is nothing to compare (rule 6), and the change column is fixed at 48px so the dashes line up even when the percentages do not.
  • Names are ink, not link-blue. The reference makes every name a blue link. Blue here would be the third colour job on a card that already has green, red and the data ramp elsewhere — say the word and they become links, but then blue has to mean "clickable" everywhere in the system.
  • 36px rows, eight of them plus the total, fills the 1×1 cell exactly. That is where --b-h-block comes from.

The tweaks

  • 1 · The hole carries the total. The reference leaves it empty, which wastes the one piece of space a ring gives you for free and leaves the card without its 27px number. A caption and a figure inside the arc satisfy rule 4 without adding a line to the card. It sits above the baseline because the chart is a half-circle — the bottom of the hole is the baseline itself.
  • 2 · Ring order flipped: biggest value on the outside. The reference puts the largest figure on the innermost ring, which is also the shortest circumference — the largest number gets the least ink, and a 74% share and a 11% share can end up drawing arcs of similar length. Outer ring for the biggest share makes length and value agree.
  • 3 · The legend is block B's row. Same .row, same three columns, same 36px band, same zebra. Two blocks that both say "thing on the left, number on the right" should not say it two different ways, and the legend was inset to the chart's width for no reason other than the reference doing it. The third column earns its keep here: the share each figure is of the total, which is what the ring is drawing.
  • 5 · It fills the cell. The card was short enough next to block B to look like it had failed to load. Same --b-h-block, and the ring is what absorbs the difference — fixed viewBox, fluid width, so a bigger ring costs nothing and the geometry below is never re-derived.
  • 4 · The hue ramp is a token, and it is not the status pair. --b-data-1..3: one hue, three depths, lightest at the centre. Three different hues would imply the rings are unrelated; green would imply on-hand stock is good news.

Kept

  • One track tint at every radius — the ring is only readable because you can see the part it did not fill. Same principle as a bar's track.
  • All three arcs start at the right foot and sweep the same way, which is the only reason the three lengths compare at a glance.
  • Each ring is a share of its own target, not of a common total — that is why they can each run past each other. The figures in the legend are exact; the arcs are for shape. A ring is a summary above a list, never the only view.
  • Plain SVG: one path per ring drawn twice, track then value, with a stroke-dasharray. No library, no JS, ~12 lines.

Using this

What it takes to put one of these on a real page.

  • Copy :root, .card / .card-*, .dots and .row / .row-* — that is the whole system, about 40 lines. Each block on top of it is 10 to 20 more.
  • Every token names its --po-* counterpart in the comment beside it. Graduating means pointing the --b-* values at the real ones and re-reading the page, not rebuilding the block.
  • The two that will argue when they land in backoffice.html: the card radius (14 here, --po-r-card is 8) and the card tone (grey-on-white here, the back office is white-on-grey today). Both are a deliberate look, so they are a decision, not a bug — settle them once, here, before the first port.
  • Nothing in this file touches styles.css, and nothing graduates by being pasted out of it.
