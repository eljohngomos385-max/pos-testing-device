  • What makes a figure soft: round bowls on the 2, 3, 6, 8 and 9, open counters, and terminals that curve instead of cutting flat. Geist cuts flat, which is why it looks engineered; the others all curve.
  • Two of the five are disqualified, and not for taste. Figtree and DM Sans ship no ₱ at all, so the browser fetches that one glyph from a system font — which is why the peso sign above looks a different weight from the digits next to it. DM Sans also has no tabular figures, so a column of amounts drifts. Measured in the browser, not assumed.
  • Inter sets everything — words (--b-font) and numbers (--b-num). Inter is the closest free match to SF Pro. Segoe UI is shown for comparison but only exists on Windows, so it can't ship to Mac or Android.
  • The weight went 550 → 560 to match: softer letterforms look lighter at the same weight.

1 · The block grid

One card is 1 unit. A card is 1 unit or 2 units and never anything else — that is the whole layout system, and it is what stops a page turning into eight different card widths.

  • 1 unit = 248px minimum, then it stretches to fill the row evenly. Four across on a laptop, two on a tablet, one on a phone — the grid reflows, the blocks never resize themselves.
  • Any block with rows in it is 2 units minimum. A list squeezed into 1 unit is where the cramped feeling came from: a product name, a timestamp, an amount and a receipt id do not fit in 248px, so everything truncates and the two columns collide.
  • A KPI card is 1 unit. The radial is 1 unit. The breakdown is 2. That is every block in the system, placed.
  • The markup is <div class="blocks"> around the cards and class="w2" on the wide ones. Nothing else.

The system

  • Striped rows instead of hairlines. Seven rules in a row is a fence; a stripe at the raised tone (#FFF on the grey card) separates just as well and disappears when you are not looking for it. Same reason Shopify's breakdown reads clean at eight rows.
  • Three columns, fixed widths on the right two. Name flexes, amount is 96px minimum, comparison is 58px. The two right columns are straight edges no matter how long the names get.
  • The comparison column keeps the em dash. Delivery charges have nothing to compare against, so they say so — not 0%.
  • Red is for the number that got worse, not the negative one. Discounts going up 12% is red even though discounts are meant to be negative; refunds going down 6% is green. The sign shows direction, the colour shows whether that direction is good.
  • The total row is just bolder — no line above it, no separate tone. It is the last stripe in the same list.
  • 2 units minimum, like every block with rows in it.

Buttons

Two shapes, both 28px tall. Rectangles do things; pill buttons choose or filter things.

  • Dropdown: one white menu, hairline, 8px corners, 28px rows. As a filter (categories, suppliers, sort) the trigger shows the choice and the choice is ticked; behind a button (More actions) it's a list of actions, with a line before the risky one. Arrow keys, Enter and Esc work; clicking outside closes it.
  • Rectangle for actions. White with a hairline by default; soft grey for quiet ones beside it; one primary per view. Hover and press are two steps darker; nothing grows or moves.
  • Pill button for choosing: filters, ranges, a status you click to narrow the table. Same fills as the table pills, 28px instead of 20, and the chosen one goes dark.
  • Both are 28px, the same as the toolbar's search and dropdowns, so a row of mixed controls lines up.

Pills

Six kinds, one shape. The same fills the app already uses for status, payment and role.
